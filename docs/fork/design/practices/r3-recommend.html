What I’d ship (orange outline below):
  • Headless Way: B, full of what passes. One open ring with nothing of its own; the sun crosses the sky, and while it passes through, the ring is full of its light, then empty again. The quietest of the three, and Harding’s point exactly: capacity, filled by whatever is there. A (a clear space with a small world in it) is the runner-up; it reads a little like a snow globe.
  • Caṅkama · Kinhin: B, one step, then the next. Each step drawn as one slow arc (lifting, moving, placing) to each half breath, the trail drifting behind. A (back and forth on a path) is truer to caṅkama but too sparse at tile size.
  • Forgiveness: A, a knot loosening. Round 1’s idea, now with a rope’s body and a still that shows the knot. B (a bowl mended with gold) is the stronger image, but it says repair, and forgiveness needn’t mean reconciliation; it’s flagged practice-only for the library.
  • The Mind Illuminated: A, the path up. The elephant path the stages descend from, abstracted: switchbacks up a mountain, growing lighter as they rise. B (scattered to steady) is the plainer, also good.
  • Anussati: B, the triple gem. Three jewels in a halo of flame, as the tradition draws them, each lit in turn. No more card suits.
  • Karuṇā: A, taking in, sending out. Tonglen without the eye: dark motes drawn in to a warm heart on the in-breath, a ring of ease going out on the out-breath.
  • Jhāna: A, light gathering in. Round 1’s gathering without the rays, on night: one soft light with no edge. B and C are the suttas’ similes (the spring-fed lake, the lotuses under water), lovely in motion, quieter as stills.
